# Retention Economics

## When to use it
Use before funding retention programmes, changing renewal terms or prioritising at-risk customer segments.

## What Claude needs
- Background from client-context.md
- Cohort retention, contraction and expansion data
- Contribution by customer segment and tenure
- Intervention costs and observed outcomes
- Renewal timing, service capacity and risk indicators

## Method
1. Define the retained unit and baseline outcome without intervention.
2. Estimate future contribution by cohort, segment and tenure.
3. Identify incremental retention or expansion attributable to each intervention.
4. Subtract delivery cost, incentives and added cost-to-serve.
5. Compare expected value, payback timing and uncertainty across interventions.
6. Set prioritisation rules, spending caps and stop or scale triggers.

## Output format
Provide a segment value table and intervention business cases with baseline, incremental contribution, cost, payback and evidence strength. Add decision rules.

## Quality checks
- Baseline retention is separated from intervention effect.
- Contribution, not revenue alone, drives value estimates.
- Selection bias and uncertain attribution are explicitly flagged.
