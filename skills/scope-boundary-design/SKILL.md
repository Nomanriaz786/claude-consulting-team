# Scope Boundary Design

## When to use it
Use before estimating work or drafting a proposal, especially where requests may expand.

## What Claude needs
- `client-context.md` for business background
- Problem statement and agreed outcomes
- Required deliverables and stakeholders
- Timing, access, capacity and dependency constraints

## Method
1. Break the engagement into MECE workstreams tied to outcomes.
2. Define included activities, deliverables, populations and time periods.
3. State explicit exclusions and client-retained responsibilities.
4. Record assumptions, dependencies, inputs and access needed.
5. Identify scope-change triggers and a simple change-control path.
6. Test boundaries against plausible edge cases and stakeholder expectations.

## Output format
Provide a scope table with workstream, included work, deliverable, exclusion, dependency and acceptance owner, followed by change rules.

## Quality checks
- Every deliverable has a clear boundary
- Exclusions do not contradict stated outcomes
- Change triggers are observable rather than subjective
