# Hiring Plan

## When to use it
Use when growth, strategy or workload changes require new people, before annual budgeting, or when hiring has been reactive.

## What Claude needs
- `client-context.md` for business background
- Current team, roles and workload pressure
- Strategy and 12-month goals
- Hiring budget and pay ranges
- Existing job descriptions and hiring process

## Method
1. Link each proposed hire to a goal, a capability gap or a measured capacity shortfall.
2. Test alternatives such as contractors, automation or redistributing work before confirming a hire.
3. Sequence hires by impact, dependency and budget, with target start dates.
4. Write a scorecard for each role: mission, three to five first-year outcomes and core competencies.
5. Design the interview loop: stages, interviewers and the competency each stage tests.
6. Estimate the full cost per hire, including recruitment, onboarding and ramp-up time.

## Output format
Provide a hiring plan table (role, rationale, priority, start date, cost, owner), role scorecards, interview loop design and a 90-day onboarding outline per role.

## Quality checks
- Every role has a stated business reason
- Interview stages test scorecard competencies, not general impressions
- Assessment criteria are job-related and applied the same way to every candidate
