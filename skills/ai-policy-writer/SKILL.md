# AI Policy Writer

## When to use it
Use when staff adopt AI tools informally, before rolling out a new AI tool, or after a data-handling concern or a client question about AI use.

## What Claude needs
- `client-context.md` for business background
- AI tools in use or planned
- Data types handled, such as client, personal or confidential data
- Client contract and regulatory obligations
- Existing IT, security and privacy policies

## Method
1. Inventory current and planned AI use cases and the data each one touches.
2. Classify data as allowed, restricted or prohibited for use with AI tools.
3. Define approved tools, the approval route for new tools, and account and security requirements.
4. Set rules for human review of AI output, accuracy checks and disclosure to clients.
5. Assign owners for the policy, incident reporting and periodic review.
6. Write a one-page staff summary with practical do and do-not examples.

## Output format
Provide a policy with scope, data rules table, approved tools list, review and disclosure rules, roles and incident process, plus a one-page staff summary.

## Quality checks
- Written in plain language staff can follow
- Consistent with existing privacy and security policies and client contracts
- Legal and regulatory questions are flagged for professional review
