# Retainer Designer

## When to use it
Use near the end of a successful project, when a client keeps asking for ad hoc help, or when revenue depends too heavily on winning new projects.

## What Claude needs
- `client-context.md` for business background
- History of work delivered and requests received
- The client's priorities and decision calendar for the next 6 to 12 months
- Your capacity, rates and minimum engagement size
- Current or previous fee arrangements

## Method
1. List the recurring decisions and problems the client will face over the next 6 to 12 months.
2. Define what the retainer includes as standing outputs and response commitments, not open-ended hours.
3. Set clear exclusions and the route for work that falls outside the retainer.
4. Build two or three tiers with distinct value, access level and monthly fee.
5. Set the minimum term, notice period, rules for unused capacity and a quarterly review.
6. Model margin and capacity load for each tier at full usage before recommending one.

## Output format
Provide a retainer outline with a tier table (inclusions, response time, monthly fee, minimum term), exclusions, change process, review cadence and a recommended tier with rationale.

## Quality checks
- Each tier delivers outputs the client can name, not just availability
- Capacity and margin hold up at full usage
- Renewal and exit terms are explicit
