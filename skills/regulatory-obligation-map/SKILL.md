# Regulatory Obligation Map

## When to use it
Use when entering a regulated activity, preparing assurance or clarifying overlapping technology and data requirements.

## What Claude needs
- `client-context.md` for business background
- Jurisdictions, activities and regulated information
- Confirmed laws, rules, contracts and adopted standards
- Existing policies, controls, evidence and owners

## Method
1. Define scope by entity, jurisdiction, service, technology and information category.
2. Decompose source requirements into discrete, testable obligations.
3. Classify obligations by governance, protection, retention, reporting and response.
4. Map each obligation to policy, control, evidence, owner and review frequency.
5. Identify gaps, duplication, conflicts and uncertain interpretations.
6. Prioritise remediation and refer legal questions to qualified advisers.

## Output format
Provide an obligations register with source, clause, requirement, applicability, control, evidence, owner, status and action. Add a scope statement and uncertainty log.

## Quality checks
- Each obligation cites an authoritative source and version date.
- Applicability logic is documented rather than assumed.
- Evidence demonstrates operation, not only policy existence.
