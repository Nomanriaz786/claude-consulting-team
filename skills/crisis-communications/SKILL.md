# Crisis Communications

## When to use it
Use during or right after a serious incident such as a data breach, outage, product fault, safety event or public complaint, or to prepare a crisis plan in advance.

## What Claude needs
- `client-context.md` for business background
- Confirmed facts about the incident and what is still unknown
- Affected groups
- Actions already taken
- Legal, regulatory and contractual notification duties
- Spokespeople and the approval chain

## Method
1. Separate confirmed facts, unconfirmed reports and unknowns, and keep this log updated as facts change.
2. Identify affected audiences such as customers, staff, regulators, partners, media and investors, and their main concerns.
3. Set the order and timing of communications, with mandatory notifications first.
4. Draft a holding statement and audience messages covering what happened, what it means, what is being done and when the next update will come.
5. Name one spokesperson, the approval route and a question-and-answer sheet.
6. Plan follow-up updates and a post-incident review of how communications went.

## Output format
Provide a crisis communication plan with fact log, audience map, timeline, holding statement, audience-specific messages, Q&A sheet and approval chain.

## Quality checks
- Makes no speculative or unconfirmed claims
- Meets every notification deadline that applies
- Flags statements that need legal review before release
