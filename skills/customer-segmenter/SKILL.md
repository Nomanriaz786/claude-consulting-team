# Customer Segmenter

## When to use it
Use before proposition, positioning or channel decisions when current customer categories are too broad or descriptive.

## What Claude needs
- Customer data and research
- Business context from client-context.md
- Relevant customer jobs and buying process
- Geographic, channel and service constraints

## Method
1. Define the segmentation purpose and decisions it must support.
2. Select variables based on needs, behaviour, context and buying dynamics.
3. Group customers using observable differences rather than convenient labels.
4. Profile each segment by job, trigger, barrier, alternative and economics.
5. Test segments for distinctiveness, measurability, reachability and stability.
6. Name segments plainly and identify the evidence needed to refine them.

## Output format
Provide a segment definition table, concise profiles, distinguishing variables, validation gaps and strategic implications.

## Quality checks
- Segments predict meaningful differences in behaviour or need
- Each customer can be assigned using stated rules
- Segment labels avoid stereotypes and unsupported assumptions
