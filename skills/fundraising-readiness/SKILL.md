# Fundraising Readiness

## When to use it
Use three to six months before raising equity or debt, before first investor meetings, or after investor feedback reveals gaps.

## What Claude needs
- `client-context.md` for business background
- Amount sought, type of instrument and intended use of funds
- Historical financials and current forecast
- Key metrics such as growth, retention, margins and unit economics
- Cap table and prior funding rounds
- Team, market and product facts

## Method
1. Test the raise against the plan: how much, which milestones it funds, and how many months of runway it buys.
2. Score readiness across story, traction, financial model, team, market evidence and legal housekeeping.
3. Build the core narrative: problem, solution, why now, traction, business model and use of funds.
4. Prepare a metrics pack with clear definitions and consistent periods.
5. List the data room contents and flag missing or inconsistent documents.
6. Draft the 15 hardest investor questions with evidence-based answers, and name the weak spots to fix first.

## Output format
Provide a readiness scorecard, narrative outline, metrics pack, data room checklist with status, investor question bank and a dated gap-closing plan.

## Quality checks
- Use of funds ties to specific milestones and runway
- Metrics reconcile with the financial statements
- Weaknesses are stated openly, not hidden
- Legal, tax and securities matters are flagged for professional advisers
